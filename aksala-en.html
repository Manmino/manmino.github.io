Manmino Scripts (Manmino Aksala-tat)

Because Manmino is intended to function as a modern auxiliary language in digital spaces, it utilizes the Latin script. However, there are two additional scripts Manmino users have created: Manmino Aksala (made by Lyu Banya), and Manmino Gowjaw Aksala (made by Nong Syengsyu). The primary goal of these scripts is to serve as visual alternatives in various artistic settings, such as calligraphy, or works of fantasy where Latin characters would not fit.

Manmino Aksala

Manmino Aksala is an abugida in the broad sense (a script in which vowels are written as marks attached to consonant letters), inspired by Brahmic scripts. Each syllable is built around a consonant letter, and vowels are written as diacritics. However, whereas in most Brahmic scripts a standalone consonant carries an implicit vowel, and vowel diacritics often come in long-short pairs, in Manmino Aksala a standalone consonant carries no vowel, and diacritics stand for either glides or vowels. Manmino vowel-initial syllables take the letter <'>/ʔ/ as the initial consonant, a feature shared by many Brahmic scripts.

The easiest way to introduce the system is through an example. If the phrase "Nyang hwa a syangsi Manmino-ne sap-lu haw i ay." were written in Manmino Aksala and back-transliterated as if it were Sanskrit text, it would look like "Niyāṅ huvā ā śāṅasī Mānamīno-ne sāpa-lū hāva ī āya." Vowel diacritics are read as long vowels (except e and o, which Sanskrit already treats as long), glide diacritics as short vowels, and unmarked letters as carrying the short a.

Because a diacritic cannot carry another diacritic, a syllable that contains both a glide and a vowel needs a consonant to hold the vowel. Manmino Aksala uses the letter matching the glide, ‹w› or ‹y›, as this link. It is written twice, once as the glide mark and once as the linker, but pronounced only once, and it plays the same role that <'> plays in vowel-initial syllables. This is why hwa surfaces as ‹huvā› and syangsi as ‹siyāṅasī›. Also, note that although Manmino Latin Aksala treats as [ɕ] as an allophone of /s/ next to /j/, in Manmino Aksala, [ɕ] receives its own letter for the sake of easier writing.
In short, Manmino Aksala keeps the Brahmic principle of attaching vowels to consonant letters, but drops the implicit vowel and adds a glide-and-linker system that sets it apart from its Brahmic models.

The consonants are designed to resemble both their counterparts in Siddham script and common Chinese characters with similar initials. Meanwhile, the vowels are designed to be simple to write, with back vowels and glides on the bottom, front vowels and glides on top, and ‹a› to the right of the consonant.

Because the writing system is designed to be a blend of Chinese and Brahmic writing systems, Manmino Aksala can be written either horizontally (going left to right and then down), or vertically (going top down and then left). If writing vertically, care should be given to ensure vowel diacritics are parsed with the correct consonant. Meanwhile, if writing horizontally care should be given to ensure ‹a› is not confused with the danda, a common punctuation in Indic scripts. Manmino Aksala can use either Sinitic or Indic punctuation or numbers, depending on the context.
